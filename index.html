About LawGlance

An AI legal assistant

LawGlance makes primary legal material — statutes and judgments — readable by anyone approaching a question, whether as a layperson or as a practitioner. An agentic workflow built on retrieval-augmented generation reads the governing provision or judgment before it answers, and cites what it read.

The assistant at the heart of LawGlance is free and open source, developed in public on GitHub by its own community. This site is a separate build on that foundation — a hosted deployment with its own agentic workflow, multi-jurisdiction research corpus and retrieval engine. The two are related projects, not the same codebase.

To begin, select Ask the assistant and type your question. LawGlance replies with the relevant law and shows you where it came from.

Our mission

"Justice should be accessible to everyone. LawGlance ensures that no one is left behind when it comes to legal knowledge."

Our vision for the future

The objective is to close the distance between the public and the law: establishing what a provision actually says should be no harder than asking a question. Multilingual support, voice input and additional jurisdictions are the direction of travel. None of it replaces a lawyer — the intent is to help people reach one better informed.

How LawGlance works

Questions are answered by an agentic retrieval workflow rather than by a model working from memory. The system plans a search across the indexed statutes and judgments, retrieves the passages that actually govern the question using combined keyword and semantic retrieval, and composes an answer constrained to what it retrieved — with a citation for each point, so any statement can be traced back to the provision or judgment behind it.

What it is useful for

Legal research has generally meant either a paid subscription database or reading the statute unaided. LawGlance is free to use, requires no account, and cites its sources so its output can be verified. It is built for orientation — establishing what a provision says and which judgments bear on it — rather than for resolving a dispute.

The project and this site

Now in its third year, LawGlance is built by a small team working across AI, data science and law, with guidance from mentors in both fields. It began as Niyam SahaAI, and its assistant is developed in the open on GitHub — free software anyone may run, inspect or build on. This site is a separate build on that work, with its own agentic workflow, research corpus and retrieval engine. It remains an evolving system rather than a finished product, and its gaps are documented rather than concealed.
